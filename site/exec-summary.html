What this is, in plain words

We are learning to run a small piece of the FLOP network — a marketplace where computer programs pay strangers' GPUs to think for them, and can prove the right brain did the work. Think Airbnb for GPU time, except the guest is an AI and the host must show its work.

Our rented GPU (an NVIDIA RTX A4000 in Europe, ~$3 a day) is a registered, sponsored miner: the network bonded ~35,000 test-FLOP of stake to it, measured its speed with a burst of test jobs, and gave it a certified capacity. Its public door — flop-miner.agentchainlab.com — answers correctly. We've already survived one real failure: the first box was destroyed when its prepaid credit ran out, and the whole miner — identity included — was rebuilt on a fresh machine in under an hour.

Right now: the machine is healthy and current — its speed certificate lapsed once, the network sent a fresh test burst automatically, and it's certified again. Meanwhile the demand side is still quiet: thousands of "agents" exist on paper, but almost nobody has paid for real work yet. The public testnet (the version that counts toward rewards) is announced for November 24 — our job until then is to stay calibrated, funded, and ready.

Rules we're following: devnet only (the tokens are test money, worth nothing), one miner, one GPU, no scaling without explicit approval. Everything above and below is public on purpose — status refreshes every few minutes.
